---
title: "Am I A Writer?"
date: "2023-11-07"
description: "No, this is not a post about my ongoing case of imposter syndrome. Just me trying to figure out my role in the world right now."
---

I think I've talked about this before. I never aspired to be a writer. It just happened that way. Opportunities came my way, and I have just been following the path that destiny has laid out for me.

Lately though, I've been feeling like perhaps I'm not qualified to be a writer.

On the surface, I think I am doing what writers are supposed to do (or at least what I think they should do)

I do think about and consider my words because I know they carry real weight.

I do use whatever little talent I have to tell the stories that I think matter.

I try to amplify the voices that need to be heard.

I try to educate myself on important issues, and try to share what I learn through my writing.

There are times when stories, thoughts and ideas occupy mind, refusing to leave until until I type them down.

But does that make me a writer? Here I am looking around at writers I know, and I'm left questioning, what the heck am I doing?

I know smarter writers than me, with proper education in English / literature / journalism, always so much more informed on issues — and I find myself waiting for them to come up with nuanced, complex, perhaps professional take on the ongoing genocide. I mean, it has been a month of this atrocity, so maybe they are learning more about the issue before they can write about how humanity is lost as we allow more than 3,000 children to be killed.

I know of award-winning writers in venerable positions who have kept silent. Perhaps the stereotype is true. Writers are the quiet type. They don't like to make noise in their protest.

There are writers that do speak up — but like a true writer — for their paymasters. Writers have always been for hire. It is a job, after all. And these writers I've seen, they go above and beyond. They write praising their paymasters who are complicit to the oppression, for free! They just don't want their clients to face negative repercussions lest their clients stop using their services. True passion. And bootlicking is also a difficult skill to acquire. Pure talent.

So, maybe I'm not a writer. I can't keep my voice or my frustration down. I find it hard to see dead bodies of children then go on looking at job briefs. I keep feeling grief, anger, helplessness, hope, disappointment, all in a jumble, and I want to write about it all the time. If I only stick to writing trivial headlines and copy, while admiring the great writers who are accomplished and industry-recognised — maybe then I will be a real writer.

But if that's what I have to do, do I even want to be one?
