## I

It's been almost two years since I last wrote anything here. There are a lot of things on my mind, but in the face of a genocide, not much is worth talking about. Maybe it's a trauma response, or it's the numbness of not being able to make a difference, but I just stopped writing. Two years. The genocide continues, the world feels like it is on the brink of collapsing, and the feeling of helplessness has grown even more.

Yet. I don't want to go gently into that good night.

## II

I saw a social post a few months ago that raised my eyebrow. A guy decided to film himself giving a cleaning lady a "flower" made from tissue paper. I'm being generous when I said "made" because it was just tissue paper twisted into a flower shape. It probably took him less than 10 seconds to make. The lady looked genuinely confused, not quite sure how to react or what to do with the flower. The post was captioned: "Be kind" or something similarly generic. I don't know if I was more annoyed at him or at the part of myself that might've done something similar once upon a time.

Since then, I kept thinking about how we often indulge in the performance of being kind. We engage in grand gestures, doing something to be seen as a nice person.

I often struggle with this thought. Every time I share a post about conflict zones. Whenever I have a conversation about it. Every time I encourage people to donate to worthy causes. Am I being performative, or am I actually making a difference?

I guess it can't be helped. A part of activism is putting on a show to increase visibility and raise awareness. It's just that a lot of thought and discretion are needed. So, while "Be Kind" is a good message to share, perhaps we need to think beyond the surface. Some questions to possibly ask:

"How can we help our community more impactfully?"

"How can we advocate for the community's well-being that goes beyond a TikTok video?"

"How can we fight for structural changes for better long-term outcomes?"

These are questions that we should grapple with every day. Not, "How can I make the cringiest content to cosplay niceness?"

## III

Somewhere along these two years, I knew I was going to be that one crazy guy who cares entirely too much. In a sea of "it's not that deep" and "nothing will change", how can I not be? Most people are happy to carry on like everything is normal. And here I am planning my conscious uncoupling from capitalism and tools and systems of oppression. Here I am trying my best to convince people that this isn't how we should live.

## IV

We always say we would be on the side of resistance. Weren't we supposed to wake up from the matrix and fight for everyone's liberation? Now that it's time for us to draw a line on the sand, it's not so easy. The truth is, when comfort is on the line, we would rather choose the status quo.

And for all my posturing, what am I really doing to push for change? Aside from reposting repressed voices on Instagram and TikTok? Yes, I'm helping to centre the voices of those directly affected and those knowledgeable on the issue, but what else?

Maybe it's time to switch up the strategy. As governments and billionaires seek to regain control of platforms and narratives, silence can be deadly. Maybe words can't end a war. But silence never stopped one either. Perhaps writing is a weak form of resistance, but not doing anything isn't really an option for me either. It is not an option for anyone. Our collective humanity would not be able to bear the heavy burden of guilt.

## V

They talk of a ceasefire. They discuss colonisation disguised as peace plans. It might divert some attention away from the destruction, but this is not the time to stop making a commotion. The struggle has been ongoing for longer than two years. It will continue longer than that. The fight is not just for one country, but for all oppressed people. Their liberation is ours, too. And if words are my weapon, then hitting my fingers on the keyboard more often is me doing the bare minimum, until I know how to do more.
