## I

When did you finally feel like a full grown-up? What was the age when you felt you had everything figured out? Maybe it was around 25, maybe sooner, perhaps later. But around that age, you begin to get a better grasp of yourself. By 30, most people perceive themselves as mature. We become more stable, more agreeable and less confrontational. This is also the age when we become less inclined to challenge our own thinking, relying mostly on our own lived experiences instead of considering new information.

## II

I recently came across a TikTok video (yes, TikTok) that hypothesises why some people stubbornly cling to their beliefs even when they recognise they may be wrong. The "Karen" phenomenon.

It all begins with a deeply held belief, which could stem from a person's upbringing, early personal experiences, institutionalised privilege, or a false sense of entitlement. Such beliefs might even arise as a result of traumatic events.

Example:

A woman could believe if she cooks and cleans, be subservient and domestic, then a man will always be by her side.

A man might go through abusive working conditions and believe that it's the only to move up in his career.

They stick to this belief because they believe in a payoff. Act a certain way, and you will reap the benefits.

When they get the space or platform, they become eager to share their beliefs. They want to be noticed, so they can get their rewards sooner. "Pick me, choose me, love *me*!" they say. They grow even more vocal the longer their rewards are delayed. At any chance they get, they will let everyone know their truth.

As they grow older, they might find their beliefs challenged. They might see proof that their belief is harmful even to themselves. The people they want still don't love them. In fact, they gain the so-called haters. Or worse, an entire generation might tell them the life they coveted and suffered for is not desirable for anyone. But at this point, it's too late to turn their backs on their beliefs. They were so loud and uncompromising, advocating for a particular lifestyle, and for what? For people to tell them there's a better way? How can they be happy knowing this?

So, they become angry. Angry at the crumbling of their belief system. Admitting this can feel like an indictment of them having wasted their life. So, they have no choice but to dig their heels in further, protesting against reality. They'll exclaim, "I've gone through abuse and trauma, but look at me, I turned fine. So, I'm right, and you're wrong". And that's how a Karen is born.

## III

Recently, I read a fascinating book called "The Intelligence Trap" by David Robson. In the book's introduction, I was introduced to the case of Kary Mullis, a biochemist who received a Nobel Prize in 1993. Despite his significant achievements, Mullis held beliefs such as being abducted by aliens, denying the link between HIV and AIDS, and advocating astrology as a cure for mental health. To some, this may seem like an odd progression. However, such instances occur more frequently than we realise. Quoting from the book:

> "...intelligence can help you to learn and recall facts, and process complex information quickly, but you also need the necessary checks and balances to apply that brainpower correctly. Without them, greater intelligence can actually make more biased in your thinking."

Being smart or intelligent does not mean you will always be right.

## IV

I'm not particularly fond of living life by adhering to quotes from famous celebrities. I simply don't believe in following the templates of other people's lives. But I think about this RuPaul quote a lot.

> "To win at life, you have to be willing to die a thousand deaths and be reborn a thousand times"

Some might interpret this quote as a willingness to abandon a past belief to follow trends (as RuPaul is fond of doing). For me, it is about the willingness to let go of what we think we know and relearn from scratch. The moment we believe we have life figured out is precisely when we should allow life to surprise us.

While I jest about belonging to the Gen Z cohort, I do genuinely hold a deep respect for the younger generation because they make me realize how little I actually know. They have demonstrated that there are alternative ways to approach life without compromising our values.

## V

Growing up was weird. Growing old is weirder. The world expects you to know and understand everything. Even when you don't, you have to pretend as if you do. I prefer to be upfront and admit it. This way, I can openly embrace opportunities for learning and personal growth, even when I'm no longer socially expected to do so.

For those who followed my previous blog, this is me, beginning from the start again.
