title: "Don't Look Away"
date: "2023-10-19"

---

## I

The past few days have been a case of me just doomscrolling Twitter and TikTok. I couldn't go through Instagram, Facebook, or LinkedIn because it felt like cognitive dissonance. Everything reminds me of the war. I watched the movie "The Creator" and saw how the oppressors dehumanise (for the lack of a better word) and then turn around to victimise themselves. Even playing Monopoly Go - that stupid, stupid app - felt disrespectful as the premise is to destroy property and rob resources to get ahead. My heart felt heavy and sad, and I couldn't pretend everything was okay. As each day passes, and as hope seems like a dying candle, I can only do this one thing. Write.

## II

One thing I've learned this week is about the Palestinian people: their lives, their stories, their hopes, their dreams. Honestly, I grew up listening to the news about the conflict. So much so that it feels like an annual occurrence. In all the news reporting (and even now, with the social media sharing), they were all portrayed as casualties. They were dead bodies to be grieved for. There were people who were crying and shouting for help, as no help came. It desensitised me a lot.

But these few days alone, I got to see glimpses of their lives in an open-air prison. Even as their oppressors try to diminish and erase them from our collective memories, they still live their lives weaving dreams into their realities. There were children with dreams of becoming a star, rapping about their lost relatives. There are authors and poets, award-winning and celebrated for their truths. There are chanteuses singing about just wanting freedom.

Seeing all these connected me to their plight more than watching the news ever did. Unfortunately, most platforms are banning these and trying to silence their voices. Don't let that happen. Read their books and poems. Listen to their songs. Watch their movies and documentaries. Bear witness to the lives they lived.

## III

One of the easiest ways for oppressors to justify their crimes is by dehumanising their victims. By framing them as a collective, you won't feel anything when watching individual lives cut short. But each and every victim has their own story. I implore you to consider this every time you scroll past, swipe, and tap forward when you see a Palestinian's story. You are not just dismissing their collective struggle but also minimising the value of their lives. Don't swipe away their final goodbyes. Screenshot or download them and keep them as historical artefacts. Not only to remember them but to remember which side of history you are on.

## IV

Oppression can happen anywhere. It might not be at the grand scale you are witnessing today, but it can happen to you, too. At the workplace, around your neighbourhood, communities, and country you live in - anywhere. The thing about oppressors is they might take different forms, but their tactics are the same. And you might think you can escape it due to whatever privilege you have; as long as there is power to be grabbed, oppression will happen. So, watch and witness what's happening, even when you can't, so you will recognise it when you see it. So that next time, you can call it out and fight against it.

## V

Everyone does activism differently. Some might not even care about it all. I am not one to tell you what to do and how to do it. But, if there is any kindness in our hearts, the least we can do is not to look away.
