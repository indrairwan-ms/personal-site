## I

In secondary school, I made the conscious decision to skip picture day. For whatever reason, I didn't want my image to be in someone's yearbook. I wasn't particularly consistent with this, but I don't think there are many images of me in secondary school. I still don't take as many pictures of myself, but I've come to like the few I do take.

## II

Earlier in my career, I was told (by a Malay) not to be like "other Malays." At that time, I remember feeling slightly offended by the offhand remark. But I still spent a good chunk of my career trying to prove I'm not the stereotype. I wanted to be The Model Malay, and I twisted and bent myself backwards in the process. It was only later in life did I realise, that The Model Malay doesn't exist. It is merely a tool of oppression designed to push you into working harder while others reap the rewards. No matter how much I proved myself, in some people's eyes, I will never be good enough.

## III

I've always been conscious of what I post. Let's just say I am mindful not to say too much. It's a lot of effort to curate a version of myself that's palatable to the masses. But recently, I read a moving piece of speech. To quote a line from it, "My silences had not protected me". While I will still hold my peace most of the time, I realise I have a platform and a voice. I should use them, even if it means people will view me differently.

## IV

It is said that Gen Z is embracing cringe. While I attribute that to being young in general, I can see how that can be liberating. The meticulously manicured aesthetic of millennials is being replaced with a preference for unfiltered, spontaneous, and even cringe-inducing content. Such is the nature of cultural evolution - the zeitgeist is forever in flux. Thus, our perception of the world around us also changes.

## V

One of the biggest lessons I've learned: there is no point in explaining myself and my ideas to people who have a limited view of themselves. While I will never escape from being perceived, I would like to hold on to the power of not caring about such people. I would not avoid putting myself out there like I used to. I will just be more at peace with what I know about myself. In the words of Lao Tzu,

> "Care about what other people think, and you will always be their prisoner."
